Does where a router is located correlate with how outdated its version is, which capabilities it runs, how much bandwidth it shares, or how long it stays up? This page answers that directly -- countries below are ranked by router count, with average version outdatedness, floodfill adoption, bandwidth tier, IPv6 adoption, and uptime shown side by side. Only countries with at least 5 observed routers are included, to avoid single-router noise dominating the percentages.
Two different "how long has it been up" metrics are shown: "Avg Uptime (h)" reflects only how long this pipeline has continuously observed a router -- for a newly discovered router that's necessarily close to zero, regardless of how long it's actually existed. "Avg Days Since Published" instead uses the router's own self-reported RouterInfo publish timestamp, a router-side signal independent of our observation window -- a more honest proxy for recent activity that doesn't require a long collection period to become meaningful.

Router Type: Floodfill vs. Regular Peer

"Router type" in I2P's own architecture is primarily this distinction -- floodfill routers store/serve netDb entries to the rest of the network, regular peers don't. (This is different from "implementation" -- Java I2P vs. i2pd vs. I2P+ -- which is not reliably determinable from passive netDb data; see the note on the Risk page.)

Does Geographic Clustering Predict Behavior?

Everything above treats geography and behavior as two separate stories. This tests whether they're actually connected: do routers grouped into the same geographic cluster also share similar risk/anomaly scores, more than chance would predict? Kruskal-Wallis H-test (non-parametric, no normality assumption) across all geo clusters with at least 5 routers, restricted to groups of that size. Effect size is epsilon-squared -- the share of the metric's variance explained by cluster membership, independent of sample size.
